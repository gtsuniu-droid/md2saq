VOMITING SAQs — QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
Five sets, 23–25 marks each.
Answers and explanations appear directly below each question or subquestion.

Each bullet under "Answer" is an acceptable answer or alternative, worth one
mark unless the marking instruction specifies the two separate points. Where
more alternatives are listed than marks available, choose only the number the
question asks for. Do not require every alternative. Accept equivalent wording
and other clinically appropriate answers. Do not award two marks for the same
point expressed twice within one subquestion.

Teaching notes explain the reasoning. They are NOT extra marking requirements.
Management is limited to MD2 principles. Therapeutic Guidelines (eTG), Australia,
was consulted directly to check management content on 20 September 2026.
Source codes beside questions link to the references at the end of each set.
These are original practice cases and marking guides, not official eTG questions.


VOM-01 | Acute vomiting and diarrhoea — questions and answers
Total: 23 marks
==============================================================================

STEM
Ella, a 24-year-old woman, presents with 18 hours of vomiting, watery diarrhoea and generalised
abdominal cramps. She has no known medical conditions and takes no regular medication. She is
alert but thirsty. Temperature is 37.6°C, pulse 104/min and blood pressure 110/70 mmHg. Her
abdomen is soft with mild generalised tenderness and no guarding. You have not yet asked about
food exposure, sick contacts or urine output.

Question 1 (3 marks)

a) State the most likely diagnosis. (1 mark)

Answer:
- Acute infectious gastroenteritis.
Marking: Award 1 mark per correct point, up to 1. Equivalent wording is accepted.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Foodborne infection or toxin-related illness is also a
reasonable working explanation. The initial label does not establish severity.

b) Write TWO history questions about exposure that could support an infectious or food-related
cause. (2 marks)

Answer:
- Has anyone around you had similar symptoms?
- Did you eat a shared or suspicious meal before becoming ill?
- Have you recently travelled or drunk untreated water?
Marking: Award 1 mark per correct point, up to 2. Equivalent wording is accepted.

Teaching note — not required for marks: One clear question earns one mark. Naming an organism or
explaining its incubation period is not required.

Question 2 (4 marks)
Two people who shared a meal with Ella are also unwell. You now assess how dehydrated she is.

List FOUR clinical findings you would assess for dehydration. You do not need to explain each
finding. (4 marks)

Answer:
- Pulse rate.
- Blood pressure, including a postural change if safe.
- Dryness of the mouth/mucous membranes.
- Capillary refill/peripheral perfusion.
- Urine output.
- Mental state.
Marking: Award 1 mark for each distinct finding, maximum 4. No rationale is required.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Assess the whole clinical picture. Urine output is
included as a clinical assessment even though it is usually obtained from history or monitoring.
Do not stand a patient who is faint or unstable.

Question 3 (4 marks)
She reports very little urine and has been unable to keep drinks down. You decide that
bedside/blood testing is appropriate.

a) Name TWO initial bedside or blood investigations. (2 marks)

Answer:
- Urea, electrolytes and creatinine (one panel).
- Capillary blood glucose.
- Full blood count, if assessing an unwell or febrile patient.
Marking: Award 1 mark per correct point, up to 2. Equivalent wording is accepted.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Routine tests are not necessary in every mild,
self-limited episode. The poor intake and low urine output justify checking for complications
here.

b) For EACH investigation you named, state ONE thing it would assess. (2 marks)

Answer:
- Renal/electrolyte panel: dehydration-related kidney injury or an electrolyte disturbance.
- Glucose: hypoglycaemia or another glucose abnormality.
- Full blood count: leukocytosis or haemoconcentration.
Marking: Award 1 mark for one correct purpose linked to each of the two tests, maximum 2.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Credit one relevant purpose for each chosen test. The
candidate does not need to give every possible result.

Question 4 (5 marks)
Ella remains alert but continues to vomit after an attempted oral rehydration trial.

a) Choose the route of fluid replacement now. Then give ONE reason for your choice. (2 marks)

Answer:
- Intravenous fluid replacement.
- She cannot retain enough oral fluid.
Marking: 1 mark for IV replacement; 1 mark for inability to retain oral fluid.

Teaching note — not required for marks: An isotonic crystalloid is usual when IV replacement is
needed. Reassess her response; a fixed volume or prescribing rate is not required for these
marks.

b) Name ONE suitable antiemetic. (1 mark)

Answer:
- Ondansetron.
- Metoclopramide.
- Prochlorperazine.
Marking: Award 1 mark per correct point, up to 1. Equivalent wording is accepted.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Choice depends on contraindications, route and local
practice. Drug dose is not required.

c) State ONE recognised adverse effect of the antiemetic you chose. (1 mark)

Answer:
- Ondansetron: constipation or QT prolongation.
- Metoclopramide: an extrapyramidal reaction such as acute dystonia.
- Prochlorperazine: an extrapyramidal reaction or sedation.
Marking: Award 1 mark for one correct adverse effect of the selected drug.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Only one adverse effect is required, and it must match
the selected medicine. QT risk is more relevant with electrolyte disturbance.

d) Give ONE reason antibiotics are not routinely needed for uncomplicated acute watery
gastroenteritis. (1 mark)

Answer:
- It is commonly self-limiting.
- Many cases are viral and antibiotics will not treat the cause.
Marking: Award 1 mark per correct point, up to 1. Equivalent wording is accepted.

Teaching note — not required for marks: This does not mean antibiotics are never indicated.
Severe systemic infection and selected bacterial infections need a different assessment.

Relevant eTG source(s): TG01, TG02, TG03, TG04.

Question 5 (5 marks)
Despite initial fluid replacement, Ella develops temperature 39.3°C, BP 86/50 mmHg and new
confusion.

a) Name the major complication that must now be considered, beyond dehydration alone. (1 mark)

Answer:
- Sepsis.
Marking: Award 1 mark per correct point, up to 1. Equivalent wording is accepted.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Hypovolaemia and sepsis can coexist. Sepsis is suspected
infection with organ dysfunction; fever or tachycardia alone would not establish it.

b) List THREE immediate management actions. (3 marks)

Answer:
- Call for urgent senior/resuscitation help.
- Give reassessed IV isotonic fluid resuscitation.
- Give prompt IV antibiotics for suspected sepsis.
- Closely monitor vital signs and urine output.
Marking: Award 1 mark per appropriate distinct action, maximum 3. No dose, detailed protocol or
rationale is required.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Clinical assessment, cultures when feasible and
treatment proceed together. Do not delay needed antibiotics simply to complete testing.

c) State ONE finding that would make loperamide inappropriate. (1 mark)

Answer:
- Bloody diarrhoea.
- High fever with suspected inflammatory/invasive diarrhoea.
- Concern for bowel obstruction or ileus.
Marking: Award 1 mark per correct point, up to 1. Equivalent wording is accepted.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Antimotility treatment is not a substitute for assessing
a worsening patient.

Relevant eTG source(s): TG05, TG03.

Question 6 (2 marks)

Before discharge after recovery, state TWO practical checks or pieces of advice. (2 marks)

Answer:
- Confirm that she can drink and retain enough fluid.
- Confirm that urine output/perfusion is improving.
- Advise return for worsening illness, blood in stool or inability to drink.
- Give hand-hygiene and relevant food-handling/work advice.
Marking: Award 1 mark per correct point, up to 2. Equivalent wording is accepted.

Teaching note — not required for marks: Choose two. A detailed written discharge plan is not
required for two marks.

Relevant eTG source(s): TG01.
